Voters who mail their ballots for the Nov. 3 election face two different deadlines depending on where they live, and a Postal Service rule change means mailing on Election Day can be riskier than it used to be.

## Two kinds of deadlines

Most states require a mailed ballot to **arrive** at the election office by Election Day. According to the [National Conference of State Legislatures](https://www.ncsl.org/elections-and-campaigns/table-11-receipt-and-postmark-deadlines-for-absentee-mail-ballots), 35 states follow that rule.

The other **15 states and the District of Columbia** count ballots that arrive after Election Day as long as they are postmarked by Election Day. NCSL lists those states as Alaska, California, Illinois, Kansas, Maryland, Massachusetts, Mississippi, Nevada, New Jersey, New York, Oregon, Texas, Virginia, Washington and West Virginia. How many days a late-arriving ballot has varies by state. NCSL also notes a pending court order involving Kansas’s grace period; Kansas voters should check the current rule with the Kansas secretary of state.

## What the Supreme Court decided

In *Watson v. Republican National Committee*, decided June 29, the Supreme Court held that the federal laws setting the date of Election Day do not stop states from counting mail ballots that are postmarked by Election Day and received afterward, [according to an analysis by the law firm Faegre Drinker](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2026/6/supreme-court-decides-watson-v-republican-national-committee-et-al). The case came from Mississippi, and the ruling reversed a decision of the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit.

The practical effect: each state’s own deadline still applies this year.

## Why the postmark may not match the day you mailed

A U.S. Postal Service rule that took effect Dec. 24, 2025, says the postmark date does not necessarily show the first day the Postal Service had the envelope, [Stateline reported](https://www.klcc.org/politics-government/2026-01-03/usps-says-mail-in-ballots-might-not-get-postmark-on-same-day-theyre-dropped-off). With fewer pickups and more processing at regional plants, a ballot dropped in a mailbox on Election Day can be postmarked a day or more later. In postmark states, that can be the difference between a ballot that counts and one that does not.

## What officials advise

- The Postal Service recommends mailing a completed ballot **at least a week before Election Day**.
- At a post office counter, voters can ask for a **free manual postmark**, which carries the date it is applied.
- Close to Election Day, election officials generally advise returning ballots to an **official drop box or vote center** instead of the mail, where the state offers them.
- Many states let voters **track** their mail ballot online.

Rules on drop boxes, tracking and deadlines differ by state. [Vote.gov](https://vote.gov/) links to each state’s election office.
